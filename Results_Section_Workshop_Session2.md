# 3. Text vs Table vs Figure Decision Framework (Synthesis 3)

| Data scenario | Best home | Why | Sources agreeing |
|---|---|---|---|
| Single key number (one prevalence, one effect) | **Text** | A sentence is faster than a table for ≤3 numbers | AMA; Editage; Enago |
| 2–3 numbers compared once | **Text** | Table overhead not justified | AMA; ICMJE |
| Baseline characteristics, many variables × groups | **Table** | Dense, exact, scannable; reader cross-checks | CONSORT 15; STROBE 14; STARD 13 |
| Multiple outcomes with estimates + CIs | **Table** | Precision + alignment beat prose | SAMPL; ICMJE; AMA |
| Participant flow (screened→enrolled→analysed) | **Figure (flow diagram)** | Branching numbers; accountability of every subject | CONSORT 13a; STROBE 13; STARD 19; PRISMA 16 |
| Time-to-event / survival | **Figure (K–M curve)** | Shows shape over time, not one number | Cohort/RCT practice; SAMPL |
| Diagnostic threshold performance | **Figure (ROC) + 2×2 table** | Curve shows trade-off; table gives raw cells | STARD; AMA |
| Subgroup effects / multiple ratios | **Figure (forest plot)** | Visual comparison of point estimates + CIs | CONSORT 18; meta-analysis practice |
| Distribution / spread of a continuous variable | **Figure (box/dot plot, NOT bar)** | Bars hide the distribution | Weissgerber 2015 (PLOS Biol) |
| Trend across many categories | **Figure (ordered bar/line)** | Pattern read at a glance | Tufte; Schriger & Cooper |
| Exact values readers may re-use | **Table** | Figures cannot be measured precisely | AMA; ICMJE |
| Anything already in a table/figure | **Cross-reference only** | "No triple-dipping" | ICMJE; AMA; Enago; Editage |

**The one-line rule to put on a slide:** *Numbers to read precisely → table. Patterns to see instantly → figure. Headlines and context → text. Nothing lives in two homes.*

# 4. Common Thread Summary (Synthesis 4)

### A. Universal principles (apply regardless of design)
1. **Results report; they do not interpret.** No "because", "suggesting", "due to", "important" — those belong in Discussion. (ICMJE; AMA; Editage; Enago)
2. **First paragraph = participant flow.** How many screened, eligible, enrolled, analysed — account for everyone. (CONSORT 13; STROBE 13; STARD 19)
3. **Every estimate gets direction + magnitude + precision (95% CI).** A point estimate without a CI is incomplete. (SAMPL; ICMJE; CONSORT 17b; STROBE 16)
4. **Exact p-values, not thresholds.** P = 0.003, not P < 0.05. Never "P = 0.000". P is an adjunct to the CI, never a substitute. (SAMPL; Greenland 2016; AMA)
5. **One primary home per finding** — text *or* table *or* figure, then cross-reference. (ICMJE; AMA)
6. **Tables and figures must be self-contained** — title, units, n, abbreviation footnotes, summary-measure definition. (AMA; ICMJE)
7. **Cite every display item in the text, in order.** No orphans. (AMA; Enago)
8. **Internal consistency** — n in text = n in table; percentages and denominators reconcile. (SAMPL; Editage)
9. **Report all pre-specified outcomes**, significant or not (selective reporting is a bias domain). (CONSORT 17; Cochrane RoB)
10. **Mirror the Methods.** Results paragraphs follow Methods order one-to-one. (Day 1 framework; Enago; Kotz & Cals 2013)

### B. Design-specific requirements
- **Cross-sectional:** prevalence with 95% CI; **adjusted** associations (aOR); design-effect/weights if cluster or complex sampling (e.g., NFHS-style). (STROBE)
- **Case-control:** **odds ratios**, not risk ratios; matched analysis (conditional logistic) if matched; exposure distribution by case/control. (STROBE)
- **Cohort:** person-time, incidence rates, follow-up completeness & attrition, HR + CI, K–M curves; acknowledge competing risks. (STROBE)
- **Diagnostic accuracy:** 2×2 cross-tabulation; sensitivity/specificity/PPV/NPV/LR with CIs; ROC + AUC; STARD flow; **account for indeterminate/missing results**. (STARD 2015)
- **RCT:** CONSORT flow with CTRI number; ITT primary + per-protocol sensitivity; absolute **and** relative effects; harms; NNT; non-inferiority margin shown graphically. (CONSORT 2010 + extensions)
- **Non-randomised intervention:** TREND items; report confounding control explicitly. (TREND)

### C. Craft-level advice (from practitioner resources)
- **Introduce Table 1 in one sentence**, then summarise only the 2–3 variables that matter; don't narrate every row. (Enago; Editage)
- **Write the Results around the figures/tables first**, then draft text to point at them — note the 1–2 key results each display carries. (Enago)
- **Handle negative results head-on**; a non-significant pre-specified outcome is a finding, not a failure. (Editage; CONSORT)
- **Table legend above; figure legend below** — and figures are "read from the bottom up". (Enago)
- **Keep methods out of Results** — no "we used a chi-square test to…"; that was Day 1's section. (Enago; Editage)
- **Build display items to journal spec early** — 300 dpi halftone, 600–1200 dpi line art, fonts embedded — to avoid a production-stage desk return. (Publisher author services)

# 5. Good / Bad Examples Collection (Phase 2)

> All examples are composed to resemble Indian resident manuscript submissions across departments. None are quotations from copyrighted papers.

## Part A — Universal Results-writing errors

**1. The data dump**
- ❌ *"The mean age was 42.3 years, 58% were male, 31% were smokers, 22% were diabetic, 40% were hypertensive, 18% had CKD, mean BMI was 26.4, mean HbA1c was 7.8%…"* (narrating all of Table 1)
- ✅ *"Participants had a mean age of 42 years and were predominantly male (58%); cardiometabolic comorbidity was common (Table 1)."*
- **Teaching note:** the table holds the detail; the text carries only the headline that frames it.

**2. Interpretation creep**
- ❌ *"Mortality was higher in the control arm because dexamethasone suppresses the harmful inflammatory cascade."*
- ✅ *"28-day mortality was lower in the dexamethasone arm (22.9% vs 25.7%)."*
- **Teaching note:** mechanism and meaning are Discussion; Results states what was observed.

**3. The naked p-value**
- ❌ *"The intervention significantly reduced infection (p < 0.05)."*
- ✅ *"The intervention reduced surgical-site infection by 6.2 percentage points (12.1% vs 18.3%; RR 0.66, 95% CI 0.48–0.91; P = 0.01)."*
- **Teaching note:** direction + effect size + CI + exact P — a P value alone reports neither how big nor how certain.

**4. Imprecise p-values**
- ❌ *"…was associated with the outcome (p < 0.05)."*
- ✅ *"…was associated with the outcome (P = 0.03)."* (AMA style: P = .03)
- **Teaching note:** report the actual value; thresholds discard information.

**5. Missing confidence intervals**
- ❌ *"The adjusted odds ratio was 2.1."*
- ✅ *"The adjusted odds ratio was 2.1 (95% CI 1.3–3.4)."*
- **Teaching note:** the CI tells the reader the precision; the point estimate alone cannot.

**6. Redundant presentation (triple-dipping)**
- ❌ Same prevalence figures in a sentence, then a table, then a bar chart.
- ✅ Prevalences in Table 2; text says *"Anaemia was the commonest morbidity (Table 2)."*
- **Teaching note:** one primary home, then cross-reference.

**7. Orphaned tables/figures**
- ❌ Table 3 appears but is never mentioned in the text.
- ✅ *"Adjusted associations are shown in Table 3."*
- **Teaching note:** every display item is called out, in numerical order.

**8. Self-contained table failure**
- ❌ Table titled "Results", columns "Group 1 / Group 2", values with no units, no n, no footnote.
- ✅ Title states what/where/when; columns labelled with n; units in headers; abbreviations footnoted; summary measure defined.
- **Teaching note:** a reader should understand a table without reading the text.

**9. Methods in Results**
- ❌ *"To compare groups we used the Mann–Whitney U test because data were skewed; the median difference was 4 days."*
- ✅ *"Median length of stay was 4 days shorter in the intervention group (8 vs 12 days; P = 0.004)."*
- **Teaching note:** the test choice was declared in Methods; Results reports the finding.

**10. Inconsistent numbers**
- ❌ Text says "n = 200 were analysed"; Table 1 totals 196; percentages sum to 103%.
- ✅ Denominators reconcile across text, tables, and the flow diagram; rounding is consistent.
- **Teaching note:** reviewers check arithmetic — internal inconsistency erodes trust in everything else.

## Part B — Design-specific Results errors

**Cross-sectional**

**11. Crude prevalence only (no CI, no design effect)**
- ❌ *"Prevalence of anaemia was 53%."* (cluster survey, unweighted)
- ✅ *"Weighted prevalence of anaemia was 53.1% (95% CI 49.8–56.4), accounting for the cluster sampling design."*
- **Teaching note:** complex samples need weights and design-adjusted CIs, or the precision is overstated.

**12. Causal language for an association**
- ❌ *"Low maternal education caused poor child nutrition."*
- ✅ *"Lower maternal education was associated with child undernutrition (aOR 2.1, 95% CI 1.3–3.4)."*
- **Teaching note:** a cross-sectional association cannot establish causation; use "associated with".

**Case-control**

**13. Reversed measure of association**
- ❌ *"The relative risk of exposure among cases was 3.2."* (RR is not estimable in case-control)
- ✅ *"Cases had higher odds of exposure (OR 3.2, 95% CI 1.9–5.4)."*
- **Teaching note:** case-control designs estimate the odds ratio, not the risk ratio.

**14. Missing matching account**
- ❌ Cases matched to controls on age/sex, but an unmatched logistic model is reported.
- ✅ *"In conditional logistic regression preserving the matched sets, the matched OR was 2.7 (95% CI 1.4–5.1)."*
- **Teaching note:** matched designs require matched analysis or the matching is wasted and estimates biased.

**Cohort**

**15. No follow-up summary**
- ❌ Jumps straight to incidence with no person-time or attrition.
- ✅ *"Median follow-up was 4.2 years (IQR 3.1–5.0); 12% were lost to follow-up, contributing 3,180 person-years."*
- **Teaching note:** the reader must know how complete and how long the follow-up was before trusting the rates.

**16. Ignoring competing risks**
- ❌ Reports cumulative incidence of fracture in an elderly cohort, ignoring deaths from other causes.
- ✅ *"Cumulative incidence of fracture, accounting for death as a competing risk, was 14.2% at 5 years."*
- **Teaching note:** in older or sick cohorts, naïve Kaplan–Meier overstates risk when competing events are common.

**Diagnostic accuracy**

**17. Sensitivity without spectrum**
- ❌ *"The rapid test had 95% sensitivity and 88% specificity."* (no description of disease spectrum or comparison group)
- ✅ *"Against culture, sensitivity was 95% (95% CI 90–98) overall but 78% in smear-negative cases (2×2 data in Table 2)."*
- **Teaching note:** accuracy varies with disease severity — report across the spectrum and give the 2×2.

**18. Missing indeterminate results**
- ❌ Reports accuracy on 280 "valid" tests, silently dropping 20 indeterminate ones.
- ✅ STARD flow accounts for all 300: 280 interpretable, 14 indeterminate, 6 technical failures; analysis states how each was handled.
- **Teaching note:** uninterpretable/missing results affect real-world performance and must be reported.

**RCT / interventional**

**19. No ITT reporting**
- ❌ Per-protocol analysis only (completers), with dropouts excluded.
- ✅ ITT as the primary analysis (everyone randomised, as randomised); per-protocol as a pre-specified sensitivity analysis.
- **Teaching note:** ITT preserves randomisation and prevents optimistic bias from selective exclusion.

**20. Selective outcome reporting**
- ❌ Reports the two significant secondary outcomes; the pre-specified primary (non-significant) is omitted.
- ✅ All pre-specified outcomes reported with estimates and CIs, significant or not, matching the CTRI registration.
- **Teaching note:** outcome-switching is detectable against the registry and is a recognised research-integrity problem.

## Part C — Good and bad tables/figures

**21. Bad Table 1 vs Good Table 1**
- ❌ *Bad:* no title; header "A vs B"; age as "42 (12)" with no SD/SE label; some rows %, some counts, some both, inconsistently; 1–3 decimal places mixed; internal vertical rules; "DM" never defined; p-values on every row of an **RCT** baseline table.
- ✅ *Good:* title states population/setting/period; columns headed with arm name and n; continuous variables as **mean ± SD** or **median (IQR)** chosen by distribution and labelled; categorical as **n (%)**; consistent decimals (one for %, appropriate sig figs for means); horizontal rules only (top, below header, bottom); abbreviation footnote; **no p-values** (RCT) or **SMD column** (observational).
- **Teaching note:** a Table 1 should be readable, distribution-appropriate, and design-correct on the p-value question.

**22. Bad figure vs Good figure**
- ❌ *Bad:* 3-D exploding pie chart, 8 slices in near-identical pastels, a legend you must cross-reference, percentages summing to 101%.
- ✅ *Good:* horizontal bar chart, categories ordered by magnitude, direct data labels, the key category highlighted in a single accent colour, no gridlines or 3-D.
- **Teaching note:** pies with many similar slices defeat comparison; an ordered bar chart with the headline highlighted passes the "squint test".

**23. Bad CONSORT diagram vs Good CONSORT diagram**
- ❌ *Bad:* three vague boxes — "Patients", "Randomised", "Analysed" — no numbers, no exclusion reasons, no per-arm split, no losses.
- ✅ *Good:* assessed for eligibility (n) → excluded with reasons (n) → randomised (n) → allocated/received per arm (n) → lost to follow-up & discontinued per arm (n, reasons) → analysed per arm (n, exclusions). CTRI number in the legend.
- **Teaching note:** the flow diagram is an accounting ledger — every participant must be traceable to a final box.

**24. Bad Results paragraph vs Good Results paragraph** (same study: cohort of post-surgical patients)
- ❌ *Bad:* *"Diabetics did much worse and clearly need tighter control. Lots of them got infected (p < 0.05) which proves glucose matters. We used logistic regression. The numbers were higher in diabetics obviously."* — interpretation, naked P, methods-in-results, vagueness, no estimates.
- ✅ *Good:* *"Surgical-site infection occurred in 38/210 patients (18.1%). Incidence was higher among patients with diabetes than without (28.0% vs 12.4%; adjusted RR 1.9, 95% CI 1.2–3.0; P = 0.006). Median follow-up was 30 days with 4% loss (Table 3)."*
- **Teaching note:** report counts with denominators, the comparison with an adjusted estimate + CI + exact P, and the follow-up — nothing else.

```r
library(tidyverse)
library(survminer)   # Kaplan–Meier
library(survival)
library(pROC)        # ROC

# Kaplan–Meier with risk table + CI bands
fit <- survfit(Surv(time, status) ~ arm, data = trial)
ggsurvplot(fit, data = trial, conf.int = TRUE, risk.table = TRUE,
           pval = TRUE, xlab = "Months", ylab = "Survival probability")

# ROC with AUC
roc_obj <- roc(response = df$disease, predictor = df$biomarker)
plot(roc_obj, print.auc = TRUE, print.thres = "best")

# Dot plot instead of a bar chart (Weissgerber principle)
ggplot(df, aes(group, value)) +
  geom_jitter(width = 0.12, alpha = 0.6) +
  stat_summary(fun = median, geom = "crossbar", width = 0.4) +
  theme_minimal()
```

**On-Screen Content:**

### Slide 9: A complete result has direction, magnitude, precision, and exact P

**Type:** Content
**Layout:** One annotated specimen sentence with callouts.
**On-Screen Content:**
- **RR 0.66 (95% CI 0.48–0.91); P = 0.01**
- Direction ✓ Magnitude ✓ Precision (CI) ✓ Exact P ✓
- P is the **adjunct**, the CI is the message
- Never "P = 0.000"; report P < 0.001
**Image/Visual:** [DIAGRAM: annotated sentence with four callout tags]
**Speaker Notes:** This is objective three made concrete. Four ingredients: which way (direction), how much (effect size), how sure (confidence interval), and the exact P value — 0.01, not "< 0.05". The interval carries the clinical message; the P value is just an adjunct. And a software quirk to kill now: there is no P = 0.000 — report it as P < 0.001. AMA style also drops the leading zero: P = .01. *Transition:* "Let's see these errors in the wild."
**Source/Attribution:** SAMPL (Lang & Altman); Greenland et al. (Eur J Epidemiol 2016); AMA 11th ed.

### Slide 14: Table 1 reports distribution-appropriate summaries — by variable type

**Type:** Content (Table principles)
**Layout:** Left rules; right a clean specimen Table 1 fragment.
**On-Screen Content:**
- Continuous, symmetric → **mean ± SD**
- Continuous, skewed → **median (IQR)**
- Categorical → **n (%)** with denominator
- Title above · footnotes below · abbreviations defined
- No internal vertical lines
**Image/Visual:** [IMAGE: well-formatted Table 1 — Phase 3 #4, or built specimen]
**Speaker Notes:** Table 1 is where design literacy shows. Match the summary measure to the variable: symmetric continuous gets mean and SD, skewed gets median and IQR — don't put a mean ± SD on length-of-stay, which is always right-skewed. Categories get n and percent, with the denominator clear. Formatting: title on top, abbreviation key and footnotes below, no vertical rules, consistent decimals. *Transition:* "And the question everyone asks — do I put p-values in Table 1?"
**Source/Attribution:** AMA 11th ed.; CONSORT Item 15; STROBE Item 14.
**Design Notes:** Photograph-worthy badge.

### Slide 15: P-values in Table 1 — no for RCTs, yes-ish for observational

**Type:** Comparison
**Layout:** Two columns: RCT | Observational.
**On-Screen Content:**
- **RCT:** NO baseline p-values (randomisation handles it) — CONSORT
- **Observational:** group comparison or **SMD** to show balance
- Why: in an RCT any baseline difference is by chance — testing it is meaningless
**Image/Visual:** [DIAGRAM: split panel]
**Speaker Notes:** This is the single most common Table 1 error in resident RCT manuscripts. In a properly randomised trial, any baseline difference *is* a chance difference — by definition — so a p-value testing it answers a question no one asked, and CONSORT discourages it. In observational work, where groups self-select, you do want to show comparability — use a standardised mean difference or a comparison, but interpret it as balance, not hypothesis-testing. *Transition:* "Same logic of fitness applies to figures."
**Source/Attribution:** CONSORT 2010 Item 15 (E&E); STROBE.
**Design Notes:** Bold "NO" / "SMD".
